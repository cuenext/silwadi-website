Digital dentistry

Technology with a clinical purpose.

Digital workflows can support assessment, planning, communication and treatment delivery in selected cases. They are used as part of clinical care, not as a replacement for examination or professional judgement.

Workflow, not decorationTechnology is most useful when it answers a clinical question or makes planning and communication clearer.

Clinical workflows

Four ways digital tools may support care.

The exact workflow depends on the treatment and individual case. Not every technology is required for every patient.

01

CAD / CAM Dentistry

Computer-aided design and manufacturing can be used in selected restorative and prosthodontic cases to support the design and production workflow for restorations such as crowns or veneers.

Clinical useSelected restorative and aesthetic workflows where digital design is clinically appropriate.
02

Intraoral Scanning

Intraoral scanning creates a digital representation of the teeth and oral structures. It may be used instead of or alongside conventional impressions for selected restorative and planning workflows.

Clinical useDigital impressions, restorative planning and communication between the clinical team and laboratory workflows.
03

Guided Implant Planning

Digital implant planning can support review of implant position and the restorative plan in selected cases. Computer-guided workflows may be considered when clinically appropriate.

Clinical useSelected implant cases where digital planning can support positioning, communication and restorative coordination.
04

Digital Smile Planning

Digital visual planning may be used to discuss proportions, restorative goals and aesthetic considerations before selected smile-focused treatment.

Clinical useCommunication and visual planning for selected aesthetic and restorative cases.

Clinical judgement first

Technology supports the plan. It does not make the diagnosis.

A digital workflow is only useful when it fits the clinical situation. Examination, diagnosis, patient priorities and professional judgement remain the basis for deciding what treatment is appropriate.

Not every case needs every tool.The dentist decides which records, scans or planning steps are clinically relevant for the individual patient.

How it fits

Digital tools across the patient journey.

The technology may change, but the clinical sequence remains familiar: assess, plan, communicate and deliver care using the information that is appropriate to the case.

01Assess

Clinical examination and diagnostic information as indicated.

02Capture

Digital scans or imaging where they add useful clinical information.

03Plan

Use the records to support restorative, implant or aesthetic planning where relevant.

04Deliver

Integrate the approved plan into treatment using the workflow appropriate to the case.
