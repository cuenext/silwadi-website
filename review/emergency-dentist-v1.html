When to consider care

Dental problems that may need urgent assessment

Call the clinic if you are unsure. Some symptoms need prompt dental attention, while certain medical emergencies should go directly to emergency services.

01

Severe tooth pain

Persistent or intense dental pain may need prompt examination and diagnostic imaging.

02

Broken tooth

A fracture, lost filling or lost crown can expose or weaken the tooth.

03

Dental swelling

Swelling can be associated with infection and should be assessed promptly.

04

Dental trauma

A knocked, displaced or fractured tooth may be time-sensitive depending on the injury.

05

Bleeding or soft-tissue injury

Oral injuries should be assessed, especially if bleeding is difficult to control.

These examples are general information only. A dentist or specialist needs to assess your individual situation before recommending treatment.

Treatment

What urgent dental care may involve

There is no single emergency treatment. The cause determines what is appropriate after examination.

01

Pain assessment

Clinical examination and imaging where indicated to identify the likely source of pain.

02

Temporary stabilisation

A temporary restoration or protective measure may be used when definitive care cannot be completed immediately.

03

Dental trauma care

Management depends on whether a tooth is chipped, displaced, avulsed or associated with other injuries.

04

Referral or specialist care

Root canal, surgical or other specialist treatment may be arranged when the diagnosis requires it.

How we think about care

Know when dental pain becomes a medical emergency.

Significant facial or neck swelling with difficulty breathing or swallowing, uncontrolled bleeding, loss of consciousness or major facial trauma needs emergency medical assessment rather than waiting for a routine dental appointment.

FAQ

Urgent dental care: common questions

Short answers to common questions. Your dentist can explain how the information applies to your own case.

Do I need an appointment for an urgent dental problem?
Call the clinic first. Availability depends on clinic hours and the clinicians working at that time.
What if I have facial swelling?
Dental swelling should be assessed promptly. If swelling affects breathing or swallowing, seek emergency medical care immediately.
What should I do if a permanent tooth is knocked out?
Handle the tooth carefully by the crown rather than the root and seek urgent dental care immediately. Time can matter in dental trauma.
Can you tell me the treatment before I arrive?
No. The dentist needs to assess the cause before recommending treatment.
