When to consider care

When an orthodontic assessment may help

Alignment concerns can affect appearance, function or both. The right next step depends on the bite and how the teeth are positioned.

01

Crowded teeth

Teeth overlap or do not have enough space to align normally.

02

Spacing

Visible gaps or spaces may be part of the orthodontic assessment.

03

Overbite or deep bite

The upper and lower teeth may overlap more than expected.

04

Crossbite or underbite

Some teeth or the jaw relationship may meet in an altered position.

05

Relapse after treatment

Teeth can move after previous orthodontic care, especially if retention changes.

These examples are general information only. A dentist or specialist needs to assess your individual situation before recommending treatment.

Treatment

Orthodontic treatment options

The appliance is chosen after diagnosis. Not every option is suitable for every bite or every patient.

01

Fixed braces

A controlled orthodontic system used to move teeth through a planned sequence of adjustments.

02

Clear aligners

Removable aligner treatment can be considered for selected cases after specialist assessment.

03

Lingual & aesthetic options

More discreet orthodontic systems may be considered when clinically appropriate and available.

04

Retention

Retainers help maintain the corrected tooth position after active orthodontic treatment.

How we think about care

Specialist planning, not one-size-fits-all orthodontics.

The goal is not simply to make teeth look straighter. Orthodontic planning considers the way the teeth meet, growth where relevant, oral health and the stability of the result.

Digital recordsScanning and imaging can support planning where appropriate.

FAQ

Orthodontics: common questions

Short answers to common questions. Your dentist can explain how the information applies to your own case.

Are braces the only option?
No. Depending on the case, options may include fixed braces, clear aligners or other orthodontic systems. A specialist assessment determines what is appropriate.
Can adults have orthodontic treatment?
Yes. Orthodontic treatment can be considered for adults as well as children and teenagers, depending on the clinical findings.
How long does orthodontic treatment take?
Treatment time varies with the bite, amount of tooth movement, appliance used and how the teeth respond. Your orthodontist can estimate this after assessment.
Will I need retainers afterwards?
Retention is commonly part of orthodontic care. Your orthodontist will explain the retention plan that applies to your case.
Can I choose Invisalign or clear aligners?
Clear aligners can be suitable for selected cases, but the orthodontist needs to assess whether they can achieve the required movements safely and predictably.
