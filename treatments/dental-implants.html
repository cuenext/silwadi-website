When to consider care

When implant treatment may be considered

Dental implants are one option for replacing missing teeth. Suitability depends on the mouth, medical history and the restoration being planned.

01

Single missing tooth

An implant-supported crown may replace one missing tooth in a suitable site.

02

Several missing teeth

Implants can sometimes support a bridge where multiple teeth are absent.

03

Unstable denture

Implants may be considered to improve support or retention for selected dentures.

04

Failing tooth

When a tooth cannot be predictably retained, replacement options can be discussed after assessment.

05

Old implant concerns

Pain, mobility, bleeding or restoration problems around an existing implant should be assessed.

These examples are general information only. A dentist or specialist needs to assess your individual situation before recommending treatment.

Treatment

Implant treatment options

The number of implants is not decided in isolation. The final restoration and the supporting tissues guide the plan.

01

Single implant crown

A single implant can support a crown when the site and surrounding tissues are suitable.

02

Implant-supported bridge

Multiple missing teeth may sometimes be restored with an implant-supported bridge.

03

Implant-supported denture

Selected removable prostheses can use implants for added support or retention.

04

Full-arch rehabilitation

Complex cases may involve a fixed or removable full-arch plan after detailed assessment.

How we think about care

Implant dentistry works best as a coordinated plan.

Implants are not simply screws placed in bone. The final tooth or prosthesis, soft tissue, bone, bite and hygiene all influence the treatment sequence and long-term result.

FAQ

Dental implants: common questions

Short answers to common questions. Your dentist can explain how the information applies to your own case.

Is everyone suitable for dental implants?
No. Suitability depends on oral health, available bone, gum condition, medical factors and the planned restoration.
Do I always need a bone graft?
No. Bone augmentation is only considered when the available bone and treatment plan indicate that it may be required.
Do all implant cases use guided surgery?
No. Digital or guided workflows may be used when they are clinically useful for the individual case.
How long does implant treatment take?
Timelines vary depending on the site, healing, whether other procedures are needed and the type of final restoration.
How do I care for a dental implant?
Implants require regular cleaning at home and professional maintenance. Your dental team will explain the cleaning approach for your specific restoration.

Considering dental implants?

Start with a clinical assessment so the team can review your restorative needs, supporting tissues and treatment options.
